## 1. Executive Summary & Epistemic Verdict

This dossier provides an isnād-cum-matn analysis of the *Jassāsah* transmission complex across 16 canonical and regional witnesses. 
- **Topological Finding**: A robust Common Link is detected at **ʿĀmir b. Sharāḥīl al-Shaʿbī** (d. ~104 AH) with a fan-out of 7 distinct transmission branches terminating in 7 independent collectors (Sahih Muslim, Sunan Abi Dawud, Jami al-Tirmidhi, Sunan Ibn Majah, Musnad Ahmad, Sunan al-Nasai, Sahih Ibn Hibban).
- **Corroborating PCLs**: Secondary transmission nodes are confirmed at **Ismāʿīl b. Abī Khālid**, **ʿAbd Allāh b. Buraydah**, **Ghaylān b. Jarīr**, **Sayyār Abū al-Ḥakam**, and **Mujālid b. Saʿīd**.
- **Historical Horizon**: The transmission stemma dates the systematic circulation of the tradition to Kufa in the late 1st century AH (circa 80–100 AH).
- **Epistemic Limitation**: The single strand (spider line) preceding al-Shaʿbī back through Fāṭimah bint Qays to the Prophet is historically uncorroborated through stemma topology alone.

## 4. Evaluation of Competing Historical Hypotheses

1. **Hypothesis A (al-Shaʿbī as First Systematic Distributor)**: Supported. Multi-branch convergence with divergent regional paths converges on al-Shaʿbī in Kufa.
2. **Hypothesis B (Pre-Shaʿbī Circulation)**: Unconfirmed. No independent non-Shaʿbī lines exhibit equivalent multi-branch fan-out.
3. **Hypothesis C (Retrospective Attribution)**: Unlikely for the core narrative due to high collector diversity and low bypass ratio (0.0625).
